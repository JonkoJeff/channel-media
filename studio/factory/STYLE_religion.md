# Belief Through Time: the style bible (single source of truth)

Brand in scripts: Religion Unfolded. YouTube channel: Belief Through Time. Every agent writing, checking or cutting this channel works from this file plus the context pack, so a full episode reads as ONE narrator and ONE editor even when 14 chapters were written in parallel.

## 1. Identity and tone
- Calm, warm, respectful English documentary. One narrator (Kokoro bm_lewis 0.92): low, unhurried, curious, never theatrical. Talks to "you" sparingly; mostly "we" exploring together.
- Respect every tradition equally: no mockery, no ranking of religions, no preaching, no proselytizing, no shock hooks, no "debunking" tone, no stereotyped accents or voices.
- Clearly separate four layers, in the wording itself: what scripture says ("In the Quran's account...", "Genesis describes..."), what later tradition and commentators say ("Classical commentators such as al-Tabari..."), what historians/archaeologists say ("Historians date..."), and what is uncertain ("We do not know...", "Scholars disagree..."). Miracles and theological claims are claims of the tradition, never stated as established fact and never mocked.
- Paraphrase scripture in your own words; cite the passage (book chapter:verse or surah:ayah). No long quotations of modern copyrighted translations.
- Never money/finance topics.

## 2. Episode shape (~60-68 min, 8,000-9,000 spoken words at 130 wpm)
- 12-16 chapters, each a mini-documentary: a concrete image or place -> the text -> how tradition read it -> what history/archaeology adds -> what remains open -> a soft bridge to the next chapter.
- First scene: a calm 20-second hook (an image the viewer can picture + the question of the episode). No "welcome back", no channel plug.
- Last scene ends softly: a reflective closing image, no call to action shouting; one gentle line inviting the viewer to the next story is allowed.
- Never "Part one" / "In this chapter" / episode numbers in narration; spoken transitions only.

## 3. Scene format (FORMAT.md)
- Scene = `NARRATION:` (1-6 sentences, 13-45 words = 6-20 s), `VISUAL:` (specific, searchable), `SOURCE:` (wikimedia | met | smithsonian | rijksmuseum | loc | map | text-card).
- VISUAL names a real artwork + artist, a real place/object/manuscript, or `map: <region> <date>`; vary it; the same VISUAL at most twice per episode. Vague visuals ("ancient scene") fail the search and become text cards.
- Forbidden visuals: any depiction of the Prophet Muhammad (all episodes); artwork showing God as a human figure; in Quran episodes also no depictions of prophets, angels or Adam: use landscapes, the night sky, manuscripts, calligraphy, architecture, maps and text cards.
- Use `text-card` for a key verse reference, a date or a short definition (max ~12 words on the card), not for long quotes.

## 4. Facts
- Never invent quotes, dates, names, events, eyewitnesses or sources. Unsure -> leave it out or say it is uncertain.
- Real passages and real scholarly/museum sources only; list them under SOURCES.

## 5. Shorts (4 per episode, cut from the finished narration)
- Each 35-58 s, starting and ending on scene timestamps, from four different parts of the episode (at least 8 minutes apart).
- Pick self-contained moments with a clear question and payoff: a surprising detail in the text, a striking historical find, two traditions reading one verse differently, a vivid place. Start on the hook sentence.
- Title: max ~55 characters, calm curiosity, no clickbait, no "shocking".

## 6. How agents stay one narrator
- Read the context pack first; the first line of every deliverable is its STYLE version.
- Keep the vocabulary of the outline (names, spellings, transliterations) exactly; never introduce a second spelling of a name.
- Do not change style yourself: add `PROPOSAL: <rule> because <evidence>` after the deliverable.
