What you see feels like a live window onto the world, but it isn't. Your brain builds it, a fraction of a second late, from scraps, guesses and expectations. In this calm, sourced deep dive we test twelve common beliefs about perception against the science: why your eyes are nothing like a camera, why you never see the present moment, why colour isn't in the objects around you, why people missed a gorilla walking through a game, why a voice can change the sound you hear, why a rubber hand can feel like your own, why pain isn't a simple signal, and why danger doesn't really slow down time. Every claim comes from named studies, with the doubts left in. At the end there is a slow, gentle wind-down, so you can listen until you drift off.
