The grass is tall and silvery, and it moves in slow waves in the faint breeze, like the sea you've just left. And all around you, in the dark, small lights are rising and falling, gold and green, drifting through the air.

@char N10 living_room_day | The fireflies | Small and slow

The whole meadow seems to be breathing out little points of light, like the quiet thoughts of the night itself.

In its glow, the grass looks silver, and the fireflies look gold, and the dark trees at the edge look like the soft, sleeping shapes of far-off hills. It's a scene so still and beautiful that you hardly dare to breathe, and then you remember that breathing is just what you're meant to do.

@char N01 cafe_sunset | The sound of the night | Crickets and wind
The meadow is full of gentle sounds. Crickets, with their slow, silvery trill. The wind in the grass, a long, soft sigh. Now and then, a distant owl, calling once, and then falling quiet again. They don't clash, or compete. They make a single, wide, calm music, the oldest music there is, and it has no beginning and no end.

@char N09 autumn_overlook | A music with no song | Just the night
It's interesting, tonight, to think about music, and the shiver it can give us, and to notice that this soft chorus of crickets and wind and owls doesn't try to do that at all. There's no build-up. No surprise. It doesn't climb, or swell, or resolve. It simply continues, on and on, a long steady plain of sound, and that, too, is a kind of beauty, the kind that helps us sleep.

Some music is for the lift, and the chills, and the catch in the throat. And some music, and some sounds, are for the opposite. For the settling, and the softening, and the slow return to calm. Both are real pleasures, and the same brain enjoys both. Tonight, it's the second kind you're hearing.
