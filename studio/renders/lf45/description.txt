You know the face, the voice, the yellow bicycle and the tomato plants, but the name has simply gone. In this calm, sourced sleep-friendly deep dive we follow one imagined woman, Marta, through a sleepless night after a street party where she could not find a neighbour's name. We look at what researchers have found about why faces feel effortless, what happens when a face is turned upside down, the brain region and single nerve cells that respond to faces, the route a face has to travel before it becomes a name, why the same word is harder to learn as a surname than as a job, whether names really are harder than faces, the tip-of-the-tongue state in 51 languages, what time and age change, why strangers' faces are harder than we think, and what we know about people who cannot recognise faces and people who never forget one. We end with a few gentle, research-based ideas for names, and then a long, slow guided wind-down to help you fall asleep.
