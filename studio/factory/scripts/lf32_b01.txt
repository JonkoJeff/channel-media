@char A15 autumn_overlook | A word about moving | Motivation
The word motivation comes from the Latin verb movere, which means to move. And that's a lovely way to think about it. Motivation is whatever moves you. It's the force that turns a wish into a step, a thought into an action, a want into a doing.

@char A03 living_room_day | A comfortable picture | A tank of fuel
Most of us carry a simple picture of motivation. A sort of fuel tank. When the tank is full, you get things done. When it's empty, you don't. And if you want more, you just need to find more fuel, or more discipline, or a bigger reason. It's a tidy picture. As you'll see tonight, it's not quite how any of this works.

@char A02 cafe_sunset | A kinder view | Not a score of character
Seen this way, a slow morning isn't a verdict on your character. It's information. It tells you which voices were loud today, and which were quiet. That's a kinder way to look at yourself, and, as we'll see tonight, it's also closer to what the research actually suggests.

@char A03 cabin_fireplace | Pushes and pulls | Two directions
Psychologists often describe two broad directions. Some forces push you from behind, like hunger, discomfort, or worry. Others pull you from in front, like a goal, a treat, or something you're curious about. Most real actions have a bit of both. You get up because staying in bed feels wrong, and because breakfast sounds good.

@char A12 autumn_overlook | Inside and outside | Two kinds of reason
There's another distinction we'll keep coming back to tonight. Sometimes you do a thing because the doing itself is enjoyable. You read a novel because you want to know what happens. Other times you do a thing for something outside it, a grade, praise, a prize, or to avoid trouble. Researchers call these intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.

@char A15 rooftop_dusk | Both are real | Neither is bad
Neither kind is good or bad on its own. Most of what we do mixes them. You might cook because you enjoy it, and also because people are hungry. But the two kinds don't always add up neatly. Sometimes, when you put them together, something unexpected happens.

@char A10 cabin_fireplace | Why it matters | The same act, two reasons
It matters because the reason behind an action can change how it feels to do it. Going for a walk because you love the evening air feels different from going for a walk because someone told you to. The steps are the same. The experience isn't, and neither, sometimes, is whether you'll want to do it again tomorrow.
