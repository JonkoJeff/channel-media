@chapter A19 cabin_fireplace | Why it feels so real
At the very beginning of tonight, you opened your eyes, and the room was simply there. Sharp, coloured, and now. Since then we've found that almost every part of that experience is built, delayed, filled in or guessed. So here is the question we left open. If it's all a guess, why doesn't it feel like one?

@char A10 living_room_day | A puzzle | The guess feels like a window
It's a real puzzle. You don't feel as though you're guessing. You don't experience the blind spot, the delay, the missing detail at the edge of your vision. You simply experience a world. A guess that feels exactly like a window onto reality.

@chapter A12 autumn_overlook | The brain as a prediction machine
To understand why, we need to look at one of the biggest ideas in brain science of the last few decades.

@study A03 cafe_sunset | Predictive processing | A family of theories in neuroscience | The brain constantly predicts its incoming signals, and uses the senses mostly to check and correct those predictions
It goes by several names, but one common one is predictive processing. The idea is that your brain isn't sitting still, waiting for information to flow in from your eyes and ears. It's constantly predicting what it is about to see, hear and feel. The signals from your senses are then used mainly to check those predictions, and to correct them where they're wrong.

@list A18 cabin_fireplace | How it works | The brain makes a best guess ;; The senses send in signals ;; Mismatches become corrections ;; The guess is updated, again and again
You can picture it as a loop. The brain makes a best guess about what is out there. The senses send in their signals. Wherever the guess and the signals disagree, that difference is sent onward as a kind of error message. The guess is updated, and the loop runs again, many times every second.

@char A13 attic_rain | We've seen it all night | The same idea, everywhere
And this one idea explains much of what we've seen tonight. The blind spot is filled with a prediction. The flash lag happens because the brain predicts where moving things will be. The dress looked different depending on what people assumed about the light. The trick playing cards looked normal because people expected normal cards. The hollow mask turned inside out because faces are expected to bulge outwards.

So perception works a little like looking out from the inside, with the brain's own expectations always leading the way, and the senses gently correcting the view.

@chapter A05 autumn_overlook | A controlled hallucination
One neuroscientist has a memorable way of putting this.

@study A10 living_room_day | Anil Seth | University of Sussex, book Being You, 2021 | Describes perception as a controlled hallucination, guided and constrained by the senses
In 2021, the British neuroscientist Anil Seth, at the University of Sussex, published a book called Being You. In it, he describes perception as a kind of controlled hallucination. It sounds startling, but the key word is controlled.

@quote A19 cabin_fireplace | Perception is a controlled hallucination | A phrase Anil Seth uses in Being You, 2021
The idea is that what you experience is generated by your brain, from the inside, much as dreams and hallucinations are. The difference is that in everyday waking life, that generated world is tied tightly to the signals coming in from the senses, and kept in check by them. A hallucination is what happens when the control slips.

@char A13 attic_rain | So why does it feel real? | The answer to our question
And here, at last, is an answer to our question. The guess feels real because it is the only world you ever have. You never see the raw signals from your eyes, the gaps, the delays, the blur. Those never reach your experience. What reaches you is always the finished best guess. There is nothing to compare it to, so it simply feels like reality.

@char A12 cafe_sunset | And it usually works | A very good guesser
And there's a second reason. Most of the time, the guess is excellent. It's good enough to let you catch a ball, cross a road, recognise a friend across a crowded room, or pour tea without spilling it. Over millions of years, brains that guessed well survived. A guess that works this well earns the feeling of being real.

@char A18 living_room_day | Surprise is the signal | When the guess fails
There's one moment when we do catch a glimpse of the guessing. It's when the guess is wrong. You reach for a step that isn't there in the dark. You pick up a carton you thought was full and it flies up, almost empty. In that little jolt of surprise, you feel the prediction failing. That jolt is the error message, arriving all at once.

@list A03 cafe_sunset | Moments the guess slips | The missing stair ;; The empty carton ;; A stranger who looked like a friend ;; A word misheard in a song
You've probably had many of these moments. The missing stair. The carton that was lighter than you expected. A stranger in a crowd who, for a second, looked exactly like a friend. A line in a song you misheard for years. Each one is a tiny window into the guessing machine at work.

@chapter A10 living_room_day | What this means for you
So what can we take from all this, besides a sense of wonder?

@list A03 cafe_sunset | Three gentle lessons | A little humility ;; A little kindness ;; A little double-checking
I'd like to suggest three gentle lessons. A little humility, a little kindness, and a little double-checking.

@char A18 cabin_fireplace | Humility | Your view is a version
Humility, because your view of the world is a version, not the only one. When you feel completely certain that you saw something, it's worth remembering that the gorilla walked right past half the people watching, and a person could be swapped behind a door without being noticed.

@char A19 living_room_day | Kindness | Others build differently
Kindness, because the people around you are building their own versions too. When someone sees a dress, a situation or a memory differently than you do, they may not be stubborn or foolish. Their brain may simply have made a different, equally reasonable guess.

#ai lf30_b15_02.jpg | Editorial still-life of two ceramic tea cups on a small wooden table by a rainy window, one cup slightly in shadow and one in warm lamplight, cosy calm mood, no people, no text, no logos

@photo lf30_b15_02.jpg | Two cups, two views
And double-checking, because when something truly matters, a quick second look, a measurement, or another person's view can catch a guess that went wrong. Radiologists, pilots and scientists build these checks into their work for exactly this reason.

@quote A10 living_room_day | Your view is a best guess, not the whole truth | A gentle thought to carry with you
If there is one sentence to carry out of tonight, perhaps it's this one. Your view is a best guess, not the whole truth. That isn't a sad thought. It's a freeing one. It means you can hold your certainties a little more lightly, stay curious about how other people see things, and be a little more patient with yourself when your own guesses turn out to be wrong.

@chapter A12 autumn_overlook | Twelve beliefs, tested
Before we let the evening slow down, let's look back at everything we've tested tonight.

@list A03 cafe_sunset | Beliefs we tested | Eyes work like a camera ;; We see the present moment ;; Colour is in the object ;; Colour words are just labels ;; We'd notice something big ;; We'd notice a change
The belief that your eyes work like a camera, when really only a tiny patch is sharp. That you see the present moment, when what you see is slightly delayed and edited. That colour lives in objects, when it's built by your brain from light. That colour words are just labels, when they may nudge how quickly we tell colours apart. That we'd notice something big, like a gorilla. And that we'd notice if something changed, like the person we're talking to.

@list A18 cabin_fireplace | And six more | Each sense works alone ;; Taste is on the tongue ;; We know where our body is ;; Pain is a simple signal ;; Danger slows down time ;; We all see the same world
Then the belief that each sense works alone, when a face can change the sound you hear. That taste happens on the tongue, when smell and sight play a huge part. That you always know where your body is, when a rubber hand can feel like your own. That pain is a simple signal, when the brain decides how much it hurts. That danger slows down time, when only the memory stretches. And that we all see the same world, when each of us builds our own.

@char A13 attic_rain | And one more | Seeing is believing
And woven through all of them, the old saying that seeing is believing. Tonight, it turns out to be closer to the other way around. Very often, believing, or at least expecting, is part of seeing.
