@chapter A10 attic_rain | A word about tomorrow
So let's start with the word itself. Procrastination comes from Latin. The first part, pro, means forward. The second part comes from crastinus, which means belonging to tomorrow. So to procrastinate is, quite literally, to move something forward into tomorrow. It's a lovely old word for a very old habit, and the habit is much older than the word.

@char A03 cafe_sunset | An ancient puzzle | Knowing better, doing otherwise
Thousands of years ago, Greek philosophers were already puzzling over something close to it. They had a word, akrasia, for acting against your own better judgment. Knowing what would be best, and quietly doing something else. Socrates and Aristotle both wrestled with how that could even happen. If we know something is good for us, why don't we just do it? It's a question we're still asking tonight.
