This audit covers **15 real channels: six direct sleep-learning references, two adjacent channels with explicit sleep episodes or tests, and seven adjacent psychology/philosophy publishers**. It does not present fifteen interchangeable direct sleep competitors. Several channels show recent tracker growth, but the available windows do not justify a fastest-growing league table. Shorts-to-long conversion is largely unverified; producing both formats is not evidence of conversion. Brain After Dark should treat these findings as packaging and format hypotheses within its warm, calm, factual American-English style, rather than permission to copy scripts, claims, art or clips.

**Observation date: October 14, 2026.** October 15 in the title is the requested report date, not a claim that tomorrow's counts were observed. Live-search evidence includes indexed YouTube text, owned destinations, transcript mirrors and third-party trackers; many pages have earlier snapshot dates. Subscriber figures are recorded only where a source displays them, and the table distinguishes a snapshot date from the date of this audit. **High** means seen directly in indexed first-party text or an owned source; **medium** means third-party metadata, mirror or partly corroborated material; **low** means an explicitly labeled inference from limited samples. No source provides private YouTube Analytics, controlled A/B tests, retention or verified conversion.

**Common verification limits apply to every profile:** thumbnail appearance is **not verified**; no thumbnail was genuinely viewed, and creator descriptions of production or suggested thumbnail text do not substitute for visual evidence. Hidden upload tags and pinned comments are **not verified**. First-30-second spoken hooks are **not verified** unless a profile explicitly describes accessible text evidence; even then, exact timing remains unverified. A visible hashtag is labeled a hashtag rather than a hidden tag. Missing playlist, Shorts or description findings mean **not verified**, not absent. These are audit limits, not claims about what a channel actually publishes.

### Contradictions between sources

**Three apparent conflicts require qualification; none establishes incompatible controlled evidence.** Watch time and satisfaction are complementary signals, and different CTRs across surfaces are different contexts, so those are not counted as contradictions.

1. **Timing: empirical association versus long-term guidance (B18–B19).** [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/) identifies engagement differences by publication slot; [YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en) does not establish a long-term timing benefit. The observational study does not establish a causal long-term conflict.
2. **Consistency versus frequency (B17).** [Scheduling guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616979?hl=en) prioritizes a dependable routine; the [performance FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en) finds no frequency/growth correlation. Audience expectations and ranking claims are distinct.
3. **Thumbnail testing versus confounded swapping (B12).** The [CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en) warns about successive changes; the [Studio announcement](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-studio-made-on-youtube-2025/) supports native experiments. The distinction is controlled tooling versus comparisons exposed to changing viewers, not a prohibition on testing.

### What this means for Brain After Dark

- **Keep the current 2.5+ hour and four-Short daily brief as a testable production plan** (B05–B07, B16–B17). Protect useful, calm content; do not extend narration merely to inflate duration. No sleep-specific success threshold was verified.
- **Measure format purpose separately** (B01, B08, B13–B15): public starts, engaged consumption, long-video minutes, active audience and cross-format overlap answer different questions. Overlap and subscriber gains do not prove conversion.
- **Give initial uploads time and connected follow-ups** (B02–B04, B25, B28). Examine discovery sources and returning behavior before drawing conclusions from a small launch sample; no fixed exposure quota was verified.
- **Judge packaging with comparable exposure** (B09–B12). Record traffic mix and impressions, use native testing when available, and interpret broader-reach CTR declines alongside watch time rather than as automatic failure.
- **Test publication slots using actual audience evidence** (B18–B20). Specify the US timezone for any evening hypothesis; keep comparison windows consistent. Do not count every daily upload as a subscriber notification.
- **Keep language and geography honest** (B20–B22). Use consistent American English and accurate English captions, then assess real geography; translations are an access/discovery choice, not a US targeting guarantee.
- **Do not depend on automatic dubbing for 150-minute uploads** (B23–B24). The current length criterion is 120 minutes. Investigate manual localization only after verifying available features and a real audience need.
- **Validate navigation before claiming it works** (B14, B25–B27). Confirm prerequisites, valid destinations and chapter structure; measure end-screen response. These are recommendations, not actions already performed on the channel.
