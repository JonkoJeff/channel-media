They're modest. A yawn isn't a verdict on your company. Your own yawns may tell you something about the shape of your day. Other people's yawns deserve a generous reading. And a tidy explanation that everybody repeats, like the one about oxygen, is always worth a second look. Let's take them one at a time, without any hurry.

@char A19 attic_rain | When someone yawns at you | The kinder reading
First, the next time someone yawns while you're speaking, remember the crowd of possible reasons. They may have woken early, or be near bedtime. The room may be warm. They may be fighting to stay with you, or they may have just seen somebody else do it. You can let it pass, or kindly ask whether they'd like a break.

@char N10 cabin_fireplace | When you yawn at someone | No need for a long apology
And when you're the one who yawns at an awkward moment, a light word is usually plenty. Sorry, long day. You haven't committed a crime. You've performed a movement that you began practising before you were born, and which is very hard to stop once it's under way. Most people understand that perfectly well, having done it themselves that very morning.

@char A19 rooftop_dusk | Reading your own pattern | A quiet clue
Second, your own pattern can be a quiet clue. The diaries found yawning gathered near waking and near sleep. If you notice yourself yawning in a steady run late in the evening, it's reasonable to take that as one more nudge towards bed. It isn't a precise gauge of tiredness, but together with heavy eyes and a wandering mind, it's worth listening to.

@list N20 cabin_fireplace | Signs the evening may be asking for rest | Runs of yawns ;; Reading the same line twice ;; Heavy eyelids ;; Small things feeling like hard work
Runs of yawns. Reading the same line twice. Eyelids that have grown heavy. Small tasks suddenly seeming rather large. None of these is proof of anything alone. Taken together, late at night, they're a fair hint that the day is complete. The washing up will, with great patience, still be there in the morning.

@char A19 bedroom_night | A word of care | When to mention it to someone
Third, a word of care. Yawning is normal. But if you find yourself yawning far more than usual for no clear reason, especially along with strong daytime sleepiness, or after starting a new medicine, it's sensible to mention it to a doctor or pharmacist. Poor sleep and some medicines can be involved, and a professional can look at your own situation. This video can't.

@char A10 rooftop_dusk | In meetings and classrooms | What might help the room
Fourth, if you're responsible for a room full of people, as Oskar is, a wave of yawns is useful information, though it doesn't tell you which kind. A short pause, a chance to stand and stretch, a cooler room or simply a change of activity are all friendly responses. None of them requires anyone to feel accused of being bored.

@quote A19 cabin_fireplace | A yawn is something a body does, before it is something a person means | A generous rule of thumb
Here's a rule of thumb that might make daily life a little easier. A yawn is something a body does, long before it's something a person means. You can almost always assume that no message was intended. If there's something your companion wants you to know, they have a much better instrument for it, namely words.

And empathy? Hold that one loosely. Some findings lean towards a link, others don't, and one pair of studies found no connection with helpful behaviour. Whatever the eventual answer, your care for people is shown in what you do for them. It doesn't depend on whether your jaw joins in when theirs drops open.
