Around the year 112, a Roman governor wrote to the emperor Trajan to ask what he should do with Christians, and the emperor's answer shaped nearly a century and a half of policy. Belief Through Time follows the persecution of the first Christians through the New Testament, Roman writers such as Tacitus, Suetonius and Pliny, and the accounts the churches kept of their martyrs. We look at the first troubles in Acts, why Roman neighbours distrusted Christians, the fire of Rome in 64 and Nero's gardens, the letter of Pliny and the reply of Trajan, Ignatius on the road to Rome, Polycarp at Smyrna, Justin, Celsus and the Alexamenos graffito, Tertullian, the martyrs of Lyon, the Scillitan martyrs and the prison diary of Perpetua, the Decian certificates, Valerian and Cyprian, the Great Persecution under Diocletian, what historians say about how many died, the myths of the Colosseum and the catacombs, and how different Christian traditions remember the martyrs.
