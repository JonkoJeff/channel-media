A few seconds of a chorus in a supermarket, and it follows you home, through dinner and all the way to bed. Why? In this calm, sourced deep dive we follow one imagined woman, Ruth, through a single evening with an earworm, and look at what psychology and neuroscience have found about involuntary musical imagery. We cover Mark Twain's 1876 story about a jingle that spread from person to person, how common earworms are (over 90% of people have one at least weekly in a large Finnish survey), how long they last in diaries, the six kinds of trigger, the melody features that make songs sticky, a 2023 experiment on repetition, the brain's hearing areas filling in silent gaps, why earworms keep the real tempo, mind wandering and too-hard puzzles, who gets them most, brain structure and the Involuntary Musical Imagery Scale, the unfinished-song idea put to the test, what people do about earworms (including cure songs), the chewing gum experiments, and a sleep laboratory study of bedtime earworms. We finish with a gentle guide and a slow wind-down. This video is for learning and is not medical advice; if repetitive unwanted thoughts or sounds cause real distress, please talk to a doctor.
