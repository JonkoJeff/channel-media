Five old friends at a restaurant table, a teapot lid lands in the sugar bowl, and nobody can breathe for laughing. Why do humans laugh? In this calm, sourced long listen we follow one imagined woman, Nadia, through the end of a dinner with friends and her rainy walk home, and look at what researchers have found. We cover Robert Provine's notebooks (fewer than one in five laughs followed anything like a joke), why we laugh about thirty times more with other people, the 1922 OKeh Laughing Record and why laugh tracks work, the tickle sounds of young apes and the 'laughing rats', why you can't tickle yourself, a baby's first laughs, what makes things funny (incongruity and benign violations), real versus polite laughter across 21 societies, how a second of shared laughter reveals friends across 24 societies, laughter and pain thresholds, nervous laughter and a brain spot that set off laughter, the 1962 laughter epidemic in Tanganyika, and laughter in every language. We finish with a few gentle takeaways and a slow wind-down. This video is for learning and is not medical advice; if laughter or mood ever feels out of your control, please talk to a doctor.
