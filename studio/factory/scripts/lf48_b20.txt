Some time later, you find yourself walking again, slowly, on a path of packed earth beside a small stream. The night is soft and mild. The moon has risen, round and pale, and it lays a thin silver road across the water. And ahead, there's a very old stone bridge, with a single gentle arch.

@char N10 attic_rain | The bridge | Built to last
Ines, the bridge inspector we've been picturing tonight, would have loved this one. The stones are fitted so carefully that no mortar shows. Each one leans on its neighbors, and they all lean together, so that the whole arch holds, and has held for centuries, through floods and frosts and many quiet footsteps. It's a lovely thing, to be held up by everything around you.

Listen to that sound. The stream has been saying the same thing for a very long time, and it never gets tired of it. The water that passes you now has come from the hills, and is on its way to the sea. It doesn't worry about the journey. It simply goes, and keeps going, the way water does.

There's something peaceful about that. The stream doesn't push, and it doesn't force. It finds the low places, and it fills them, and it moves on. Over time, it has shaped the whole valley. And all it has ever done is flow, one soft moment after another.
