Let's gather up what we found along the way. A tear isn't simply a tear; there are different kinds for different jobs. The toxin story is probably a myth. @char A13 bedroom_night | And the rest | Signals, culture and joy
Tears are far from private. They make sadness clearer, they're read in a fraction of a second, and they tend to draw kindness rather than contempt. Whether they also carry a chemical signal is genuinely unsettled. Women do cry more often than men, but culture shapes that gap a great deal. Tears can come from joy as well as grief. And not crying doesn't mean not feeling.

@char A10 autumn_overlook | The bigger picture | Why humans cry
If there's one idea that ties all of this together, it's that crying seems to be mostly about other people. It begins as a baby's call for help. It grows into a quiet, close-range signal that says I'm struggling, or I'm overwhelmed, or this matters to me. And its soothing power often seems to come from the people who answer it. We may cry alone, but tears seem made for company.
