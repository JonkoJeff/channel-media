@chapter A13 rooftop_dusk | What it all adds up to
So let's step back and look at the whole night. We began with a simple puzzle: how can a dream feel exactly as real as being awake, and then turn out to be nothing at all? We have followed that question through the sleeping brain, and we have met studies, doubts and a few surprises. Now it is time to gather what is worth keeping.

@char A12 cafe_sunset | The first idea | A world, running offline
Here is the first thing to carry with you. A dream is a world that your brain builds, even though your eyes are closed and the room is dark. The researcher Antti Revonsuo, in a paper published in 2000, described the dreaming brain as producing a complex model of the world. That is the same kind of work it does all day, and it is why a dream can feel like life, right up to the moment you wake.

@study A04 conspiracy_board | Maquet et al. | Nature, 1996 | Functional neuroanatomy of human rapid-eye-movement sleep and dreaming
Maquet and his colleagues scanned seven people during REM sleep. They found that blood flow rose in areas linked to emotion, such as the amygdala, and fell in a large part of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, a region involved in careful thinking. In plain terms, the feeling machinery was busy while some of the judging machinery was quiet.

@char A19 attic_rain | The second idea | The strangeness is a feature
That is the second thing to carry with you. The oddness of dreams, the sudden changes of place and the people who are two people at once, may be what a quieter prefrontal cortex feels like from the inside. You do not stop to question it, because the part of you that would question it is resting. It is not a flaw in you. It is simply how this state of the brain works.

@list A03 living_room_day | What to take with you | A dream is the brain building a world ;; The strangeness comes from a quieter judging brain ;; Forgetting a dream is normal ;; A few words, soon after waking, can keep a thread
Four small ideas, then. None of them asks you to do anything, or to change anything. They are simply a gentler way to think about the odd theatre that runs every night inside your head, and about the mornings when it leaves nothing behind.

@char A15 cabin_fireplace | The third idea | You do not have to remember
It is worth saying again that nobody needs to remember their dreams. The research we looked at suggests that people who recall more tend to wake briefly more often, and to differ a little in how their brains attend to the world. That is a difference, and not a prize. Your dreams do their quiet work, whether or not you ever carry them into the morning.

@char A10 autumn_overlook | A small practice | If you are curious
But if you are curious, there is a kind and simple practice. Keep a notebook within reach, and when you wake, lie still for a moment and ask yourself what was just happening. Write down a place, a person, a feeling, only a few words. The studies were honest that a diary is no magic trick, and that it can feel like homework. So keep it light, and let it stay a pleasure.

A small light and a notebook, nothing more. That is all a dream diary needs to be. Some people fill pages with it, and others write a single word and are content. Either way, it is a way of saying to the night that you were paying attention, and that you are glad of whatever it gave you.

@char A18 cabin_fireplace | Back to the beginning | That racing heart
Do you remember where we started? That moment of waking with your heart racing, sure that something terrible, or wonderful, had just happened. Now you know a little about why. Your brain had built a world, and its emotional machinery had been fully awake inside it, while the part that checks and doubts had been quiet. Your heart was responding to something that felt real, even though it never was.

@char A13 living_room_day | A kinder view | There was nothing to fear
It helps to know that, the next time it happens. A racing heart after a dream is not a warning, and it is not a sign that something is wrong with you. It is the body following the story that the brain was telling, a little behind, as the world of the dream falls away. You can take a slow breath, feel the room come back, and let the feeling settle.

@list A05 cafe_sunset | The night, in review | REM sleep, found in 1953 ;; Dreams can happen outside REM too ;; The brain builds a world, and a quiet prefrontal cortex accepts it ;; Dreams fade, and that is normal
Here, in the briefest outline, is where we have been. A strange eye movement was seen in sleeping people, and a whole science grew from it. Dreaming turned out to be wider than REM, the dream world turned out to be built from the same machinery as the waking one, and forgetting turned out to be the usual ending.
