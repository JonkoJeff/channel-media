Because what reaches your eye is not a picture at all.

@char A03 living_room_day | Just light | Tiny particles of energy
What reaches your eye is light, arriving at different strengths and different wavelengths from every direction. At the back of your eye, a thin layer of cells called the retina catches that light and turns it into tiny electrical signals. And from that moment on, there is no image anywhere in your head. There are only signals, flickering along nerve fibres.

@list A13 attic_rain | What your brain receives | Electrical signals ;; From two small, flat surfaces ;; Upside down and reversed ;; Full of gaps and blur
Think about what your brain actually has to work with. Electrical signals. From two small, curved surfaces at the back of your eyes. Projected upside down and left to right, the way a lens always does. And, as we'll see in a moment, full of gaps and blur. From that, your brain has to build a steady, three dimensional, colourful room.

@char A04 bedroom_night | A hard puzzle | Many worlds fit the same light
Here is why that's so hard. The same pattern of light on your retina could come from many different worlds. A small coin held close to your face and a large plate far away can throw almost exactly the same shape onto your eye. A white wall in shadow and a grey wall in sunlight can send back almost the same light. The signal alone can't tell you which is true.

@quote A10 cabin_fireplace | Perception is an unconscious inference | Hermann von Helmholtz, 19th century
In the nineteenth century, the German scientist Hermann von Helmholtz put it in a way that still shapes the field today. He suggested that perception is a kind of unconscious inference. Your brain takes the incomplete evidence from your senses, adds what it has learned about the world, and arrives at its best conclusion, all without you being aware that any reasoning took place. We will meet his idea again later tonight.

@char A13 attic_rain | Eyes like cameras | The most common idea
It's the idea that your eyes work like a camera.
