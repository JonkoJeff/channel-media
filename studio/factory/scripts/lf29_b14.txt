@chapter A05 autumn_overlook | Self-compassion
There's a name for this kind of warmth towards yourself. Researchers call it self-compassion.

@study A03 living_room_day | Kristin Neff | University of Texas at Austin, 2003 | Defined self-compassion as self-kindness, a sense of common humanity and mindfulness
In 2003, the psychologist Kristin Neff, at the University of Texas at Austin, published a paper describing self-compassion and how it might be measured. She described it as having three parts, which fit rather beautifully with everything we've talked about tonight.

@list A12 cafe_sunset | Three parts of self-compassion | Self-kindness, not harsh judgement ;; Common humanity, not isolation ;; Mindfulness, not over-identification
The first part is self-kindness, being warm and understanding towards yourself when things go wrong, instead of harshly judging yourself. The second is common humanity, remembering that struggling and making mistakes is part of being human, something everyone goes through, rather than something that cuts you off from others. And the third is mindfulness, noticing painful thoughts and feelings in a balanced way, without being swept away by them.

@char A10 cabin_fireplace | Kindness | A softer tone
Let's take each part slowly. The first is kindness. You've already practised it, by speaking to yourself as you'd speak to a friend. Let your inner voice keep that tone for a little while. Warm. Patient. Unhurried. Like someone sitting at the end of your bed, keeping you company.

@char A13 attic_rain | Common humanity | You're not alone
The second is common humanity. Tonight, all around the world, millions of people are lying in the dark going over their day. Some are worried about the same kind of things you are. Some said something they regret. Some are tired, or lonely, or unsure. You're not the only one. You're part of a very large, very human crowd, all trying to rest.

The third part is mindfulness. That means noticing your thoughts and feelings without getting lost in them. You did this earlier, when you named a feeling and let it sit. You can do it again now. If a worried thought comes, you can notice it, the way you'd notice a car passing outside. There goes a worry. And let it pass.
