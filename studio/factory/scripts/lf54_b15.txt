@char N20 attic_rain | Does it travel across species? | A question for dog owners
He catches it, naturally. But the more interesting question runs the other way. When a person yawns, can a dog catch it? Dogs have lived alongside humans for thousands of years, and they're famously attentive to our faces, our voices and our moods. If any animal were going to pick up our yawns, a dog would be a reasonable candidate.

@study N27 cabin_fireplace | Ramiro Joly-Mascheroni, Atsushi Senju and Alex Shepherd | Biology Letters, published 2008 | A stranger yawns at a dog
In 2008, Ramiro Joly-Mascheroni, Atsushi Senju and Alex Shepherd tested twenty-nine dogs. In one session, a person unfamiliar to the dog yawned while facing it, again and again, for five minutes. In a comparison session, the same sort of person made mouth movements, opening and closing, without the features of a real yawn.

@bars A12 rooftop_dusk | Dogs that yawned, out of 29 | dogs | Human yawning:21 ;; Mouth movements:0
The result was dramatic. Twenty-one of the twenty-nine dogs, about seventy-two percent, yawned while the person was yawning. None yawned during the comparison session. That's a higher proportion than is often seen in adult humans watching videos, and it was widely reported as the first evidence that a yawn could pass from our species to another.

@char A10 living_room_day | Hold on a moment | Other teams, other results
It's the kind of result that asks to be repeated, and repetitions have been mixed. Some later studies found much weaker effects, and others had difficulty finding contagious yawning in dogs at all. Researchers have discussed whether stress might explain part of it, since dogs sometimes yawn when they're uneasy, and a stranger yawning directly at them is a slightly odd experience.

@study N09 cafe_sunset | Elainie Madsen and Tomas Persson | Animal Cognition | Yawning in puppies
Elainie Madsen and Tomas Persson in Sweden asked a developmental question, much like the one asked about children. They tested thirty-five young dogs, from about four to fourteen months old. A person, either their familiar owner or an unfamiliar researcher, yawned at them in one phase and made a plain gaping movement in another.

@list N10 autumn_overlook | What the puppy study found | Older puppies yawned more to yawns than to gapes ;; Younger ones showed no clear difference ;; Owner or stranger made no reliable difference
Among the older young dogs, from about seven months, yawns drew more yawning than plain gapes did. Among the younger ones, from four to six months, the difference wasn't reliable, partly because they also responded to the gapes. And whether the person was their owner or a stranger didn't make a clear difference in this study.

@char N01 bedroom_night | A growing ability | Echoes of childhood
That suggests the tuned response, to yawns in particular, may sharpen as a dog grows up, a little like the change we saw in children around the age of four. The authors were careful to say that the youngest dogs might respond to open mouths in a more general way at first. Development shapes this behaviour, in at least two species.

@char A03 attic_rain | Try it gently | With a pinch of salt
You're welcome to yawn near your own dog and see what happens. It's harmless if you do it gently and don't loom. Just remember what a single kitchen trial can't tell you. A dog may yawn because it's late, because it's mildly unsure why you're staring, or because dogs yawn quite often anyway. One yawn proves very little.

@char N29 cabin_fireplace | Cats and others | The jury is out
Cat owners often ask about their own companions. Cats certainly yawn, generously and with great style. Whether they catch our yawns is much less studied, and I don't know of firm evidence either way. If your cat yawns back at you, enjoy the moment. If it looks at you with complete indifference, that is also very much in character.

@list A10 cafe_sunset | Dogs in brief | One 2008 study: 21 of 29 yawned to a human ;; Later results have been mixed ;; Puppies seemed to grow into it ;; Stress and plain timing can confuse the picture
So for dogs: one well-known study found most of its twenty-nine dogs yawning when a person did. Later research hasn't always agreed. A study of young dogs suggested a response specific to yawns develops with age. And any yawn from any dog can have several causes, which keeps the researchers honest.

@char N09 living_room_day | The long view | What the animals add
Taken together, the animals add something to our evening. The spontaneous yawn is very widespread and probably very old. The contagious yawn seems rarer, shows up in some highly social creatures, tends to develop with growing up, and in several observations favours companions. That's consistent with a social side to yawning, while leaving its exact job open.
