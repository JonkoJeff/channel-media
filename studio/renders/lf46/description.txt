You walked into a room, met a stranger, and felt a verdict arrive before a single useful fact did. Cold, warm, capable, kind. In this calm, sourced, sleep-friendly deep dive we follow one imagined man, Felix, through the night after his first day at a new job, as he tries to work out why he decided about two people he barely knew within seconds. Along the way we look at what researchers have found about impressions from a face seen for a tenth of a second, impressions from a few hundredths of a second, thin slices of silent video and of a single spoken word, what a face seems to promise about a person's character, how one word can become a hinge for a whole impression, how the order of information changes who a person seems to be, the halo effect, what happens when people are asked why they liked someone, how accurate appearance-based guesses really are, why one person gives many different impressions, and when a first impression can change. We finish with a few gentle, research-based ideas and then a long, slow guided wind-down to help you fall asleep.
