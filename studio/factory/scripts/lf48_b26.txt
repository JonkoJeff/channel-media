@chapter N01 attic_rain | An attic room and a music box
The meadow lifts away, softly, like a blanket being drawn upward, and you're in a small attic room, with a sloping ceiling and a round window, and rain falling softly against the glass. The room is warm and dim. A narrow bed with a thick quilt stands under the window, and beside it, on a little wooden table, a music box.

@char N10 cabin_fireplace | The music box | Old and small
It's small and old, made of dark polished wood, with a tiny brass key on one side. The lid is open, and inside, you can see the little metal comb, and the slowly turning cylinder, with its rows of tiny pins. When the cylinder turns, the pins pluck the comb, one by one, and a tune comes out, as delicate as frost.

Someone has just wound it, because the cylinder is turning, slowly, and the tune is filling the room. It's a simple, gentle melody, only a handful of notes, repeated again and again, each time a little more slowly, as the spring unwinds. You don't know where you've heard it before. Perhaps nowhere. Perhaps everywhere.

@char N20 attic_rain | The tune | Simple and slow
Notice how simple it is. There's no big build-up. No sudden turn. No dramatic key change. Just a few soft notes, falling gently into place, in a pattern so familiar that you can almost sing it before it arrives. And that's exactly the point. Tonight, it isn't surprise you need. It's the comfort of knowing what comes next.

@char N09 cabin_fireplace | Predictable on purpose | A gentle truth
It's worth noticing, since we've talked so much tonight about expectation, that lullabies lean the other way. They're slow, repetitive, soft and predictable, and that's what makes them so soothing. The brain, with nothing to predict wrongly, has nothing to be startled by. It can put down its guessing, and rest.

@char N01 attic_rain | The slowing | As the spring unwinds
The tune is slowing now, as the spring runs down. The notes are further apart. Each one hangs a little longer in the air, shimmering, before the next one arrives. It's like a very old clock, or a heartbeat, gently coming to rest. You find that your own breathing is slowing too, to match.

@char N10 attic_rain | The rain | On the round window
Behind the music, there's the rain, tapping lightly on the round window, in no rhythm at all. The two sounds weave together, the tidy little melody and the loose, scattered rain, and make a texture so soft and layered that it feels like a blanket for the ears.

@char N20 cabin_fireplace | The quilt | Heavy and warm
You pull the quilt up under your chin. It's patchwork, made of a hundred different fabrics, all soft with washing, and it smells faintly of cedar and sunshine. Each little square has been stitched by someone who cared, and you can feel that care in the weight of it, settled over you.

@char N09 attic_rain | The last notes | One by one
The music box is down to its last notes now. One. A long pause. Another. A longer pause. The tune has almost stopped, but it hasn't quite, and the silence between the notes is as lovely as the notes themselves, like the space between stars.

@char N01 cabin_fireplace | And then | A soft silence
And then the last note falls, and fades, and there's a silence, soft and round, filled only with the sound of the rain. It isn't empty. It's full of everything you've heard tonight, settled and quiet, like the bottom of a very deep, very calm lake.

@char N10 attic_rain | Resting in the quiet | Nothing more
So you rest in that quiet, in the attic, under the quilt, with the rain on the glass. You don't need another tune. You don't need another thought. You only need this: the dark, and the warmth, and the slow, steady sound of your own breathing.
