Why can you feel lonely in a full room, and what does that feeling actually do to your brain and body? In this calm, sourced deep dive we follow one imagined man, Tomas, through a quiet stretch of weeks in his city flat, and take ten common myths about loneliness one by one: that lonely means alone, that it is just a feeling, that social pain isn't real pain, that more people around is the cure, that it only touches your mood, the famous 15 cigarettes comparison, that only older people are lonely, that social media is the whole story, that loneliness is who you are, and that the fix is simply to go out more. We look at what the research really shows about the brain's alarm for social threat, sleep, the body's stress systems, how long friendships take, and why small moments of connection matter. The last part is a long, gentle wind-down to help you fall asleep. If loneliness feels heavy or long-lasting, talking to a doctor or counsellor can help.
