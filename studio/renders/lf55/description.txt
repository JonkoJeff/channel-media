A grandfather's eyes fill at the last song of a school concert, and he isn't even sad. Why do humans cry? In this calm, sourced long listen we walk home along a canal with one imagined man, Hugo, and test ten common beliefs about tears against what researchers have found. We cover the three kinds of tears and the onion enzyme found in 2002, the idea that tears flush out stress hormones, whether animals weep (and a 2022 study of dogs reunited with their owners), how newborns cry with the melody of their mother's language, whether a good cry really helps (memory surveys, a diary of 1,004 cries and the ninety minute turn), what tears signal to other people, warmth versus competence across 41 countries, the disputed chemical signal in tears, men, women and culture in 37 countries, tears of joy and being moved, and people who cannot cry. We finish with a few gentle takeaways and a slow wind-down. This video is for learning and is not medical advice; if you are crying far more than usual, or feel low or unable to cry for weeks, please talk to a doctor.
