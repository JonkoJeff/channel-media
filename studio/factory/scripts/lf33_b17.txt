@char A19 bedroom_night | The real lesson | How, not what
Because the point of her story was never what she should choose. It was how a mind like hers, like yours, like everyone's, actually goes about choosing. And now that we've followed her through one long evening, let's gather together what we've found.

@list A10 cafe_sunset | What we found tonight | Two speeds of thinking ;; Feelings are part of reason ;; Shortcuts and frames bend judgement ;; Defaults pull hard ;; The gut needs practice ;; We explain afterwards ;; We adapt more than we expect
Here's what we found tonight. We think at two speeds, fast and slow, and need both. Feelings aren't the enemy of reason, but part of how reasons ever settle. Shortcuts, anchors and frames bend our judgement in predictable ways. Defaults pull harder than we think. The gut is wise where it has had practice. We often explain our choices afterwards. And we adapt to life's changes far better than we expect.

@char A13 living_room_day | Fast and slow | Knowing when
The first lesson is about speed. Most of your decisions are fast, and most of them are fine. The skill isn't to slow down everything. It's to notice the few moments when a choice really matters, and the first answer arrived a little too easily. Those are the moments to stop and check the lily pads.

@char A15 rooftop_dusk | Feelings | Part of the machinery
The second lesson is about feeling. Remember Elliot, who could reason perfectly and still couldn't choose a way to sort his papers. Feelings are how a mind decides what matters. They can be borrowed from a bad day or a rainy evening, so it's worth asking where they come from. But they deserve a seat at the table.

@char A20 cafe_sunset | Shortcuts and frames | Turning the question
The third lesson is about shortcuts. A vivid story, a first number, the words in a question, an option already chosen for you. Each of them can tilt a choice without you noticing. And often, the simplest fix is to turn the question around. Say it as a gain, then as a loss. Imagine a different starting point.

@char A12 autumn_overlook | Our reasons | Held lightly
The fourth lesson is about reasons. We saw people explain choices they had never made, and shoppers who praised the knit of a stocking when it was really its place on the table. Our reasons are our best guess about ourselves. They're worth listening to, and worth holding lightly.

@char A03 cabin_fireplace | The future | Kinder than we fear
And the last lesson is perhaps the kindest. We tend to overestimate how much any single choice will change how we feel, and for how long. The terrible outcome is usually less terrible. The wonderful one less wonderful. Life drifts back towards the middle, and our quiet inner helper is always at work.

@char A19 bedroom_night | Back to the beginning | Two words on a page
Do you remember how we began? A kitchen table, a cup of tea, and a sheet of paper with just two words on it. Stay, and go. It looked like a simple list. But underneath it, as we've seen, was a whole quiet orchestra. Fast thoughts and slow ones, feelings and memories, other people's voices, and stories told afterwards.

@char A10 living_room_day | Your own columns | Whatever they are
Perhaps you have your own two columns somewhere tonight. A choice you've been carrying around. If so, there's no need to solve it now. Let it rest. It will still be there in the morning, and you'll see it a little differently in the daylight. That's not a failure of decision. That's simply how good decisions are made.
