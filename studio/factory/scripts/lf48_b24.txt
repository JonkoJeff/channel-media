You wake, if you can call it waking, to the sound of a long, slow wave. You're lying on soft, pale sand, warm underneath and cool on top, near the edge of a wide, empty beach. The sky overhead is a deep velvet blue, wide and clear, scattered with stars right down to the horizon. And the sea is whispering, over and over, the oldest word there is.

@char N20 autumn_overlook | The sand | Fine and warm
The sand is fine as flour, and it holds the warmth of the day, so that wherever your body touches it, there's a gentle heat. It shapes itself around you, as you lie there, taking the curve of your back, your shoulders, the backs of your legs. You sink into it by just a little, like pressing into a very soft pillow, and the sand is perfectly happy to hold you for as long as you like.

@char N01 cafe_sunset | The wave | Arriving and leaving

It spreads out thinly across the sand, glimmering for an instant, and then it slips back, and the next wave is already on its way.

The sea has been doing this for longer than there have been people to watch, and it will do it all night. You only need to go along with it, and let it breathe for you.

If you listen closely, it sounds almost like a soft voice saying, let go. Let go. And with each withdrawal, you feel a little more of the day drawing out of you, back into the sea, where it dissolves.

You couldn't lift a single finger, and you wouldn't want to. It's a delicious, sleepy heaviness, like being buried in warm, soft, fine snow.
