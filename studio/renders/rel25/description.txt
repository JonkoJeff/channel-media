How does the Quran describe the sun, the moon and the stars? Belief Through Time gathers the verses scattered across many surahs: the sun as a lamp and the moon as a light (10:5, 71:16, 25:61), the moon's stations and the old date stalk (36:39), each swimming in an orbit (21:33, 36:40), the sun running to its resting place (36:38), the stars as lamps, protection and guides (37:6, 67:5, 6:97, 16:16), and Abraham's words: I do not love things that set (6:76). We follow how classical commentators such as al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir read these passages, how they shaped Muslim practice (the crescent that opens Ramadan, the Hijri calendar, prayer times by the sun's shadow, the eclipse prayer, the qibla), how astronomers from al-Battani and al-Sufi to Ulugh Beg and Ibn al-Shatir studied the sky, and what modern astronomy reports, keeping scripture, interpretation, history and science distinct. Scripture is paraphrased; interpretations are attributed to the scholars who held them. Narration is AI-generated (synthetic voice); images are public-domain or openly licensed and illustrative only.
